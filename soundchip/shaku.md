# shaku.z80s — design notes

A shakuhachi out of one SAA1099: **1,554 T-states a frame, 1.3% of a
50 Hz frame**, and no samples anywhere near it.

Verified against `soundchip/tests/shaku.py` the way everything else here is —
**every OUT, in order, register and value, 3,937 of them** — and then
that captured stream is played through `soundchip/tests/saa1099.py` to make
`demo/shakuhachi.wav`.

## Interface

| symbol | |
|---|---|
| `sk_init` | set the chip up for the flute, once |
| `sk_play` | start the phrase |
| `sk_frame` | one 50 Hz frame of it |

Needs `saa.z80s` (the two ports and the send loop) and
`shakudata.z80s` (generated by `soundchip/tests/mkshakudata.py`).

## Breath, vibrato, and a sagging pitch

A 1.8-shaku flute is bored for D F G A C D, and what makes it sound
like bamboo rather than an organ is breath. Three things carry that:

**The breath is a noise channel.** Noise generator 1 in **mode 3** —
clocked from channel 3's own tone generator rather than one of the
three fixed rates — sits under the note the whole time, loud at the
attack and about a third of the tone afterwards. Channel 3 has its tone
disabled, so that generator does nothing but set the noise's colour:
3.2 kHz, which is air rather than hiss. It is most of the instrument.

**The vibrato is the frame rate.** A shakuhachi's *yuri* is about 4 Hz,
and 4 Hz at fifty frames a second is twelve frames a cycle — so unlike
`crow.z80s`'s 40 Hz roughness, this is a modulation the CPU *can* draw.
It arrives over four cycles, nothing to ±25 cents, which is what a
player does with their head rather than their fingers. Two tables and
no arithmetic: forty-eight frames of it coming in, then one cycle
for ever, with a wrapping counter.

**The pitch sags as the breath goes.** The release table takes the note
six units down over its last frames — *meri*, the flattening that comes
of dropping the chin — and the attack starts three units under and
rises into the note. Measured on the wav: **4.2 Hz, ±23 cents**, and
every note of the phrase within **16 cents of the score**.

## What a frame does

`sk_env` is thirty-two rows of (tone level, breath level, pitch offset)
indexed by frames since the note started and **held at the last row**,
so one table serves notes of any length; `sk_rel` is nine more rows
indexed by frames remaining. The tone is three channels — f, 2f and 4f
at 0, −2 and −6 levels — and **an octave up is the same frequency byte
with the octave register one higher**, so one number feeds all three,
and the vibrato and the meri only have to be worked out once.

A frame is seven register writes, or nine on the first frame of a note
when the octaves go too. A rest writes four zeros once and then nothing
at all.

## What it costs

| | T-states | |
|---|---|---|
| `sk_frame`, a note going | **1,554** | 1.3% of a 50 Hz frame |
| `sk_frame`, a note starting | 1,990 | the octave registers as well |
| `sk_init` | 1,206 | fourteen registers, once |

Tables are 21 bytes of notes, 96 of envelope, 27 of release, 60 of
vibrato and 30 of phrase; code is about 300.

## Invariants

- Every byte the routine writes must land in 0..255 and it does not
  check: `soundchip/tests/shaku.py` asserts the note table against the vibrato
  depth and the largest bend, at both ends.
- The envelope tables are three bytes a row and the code multiplies the
  index by three; the vibrato tables are signed bytes.
- `sk_k` saturates rather than wrapping, because the envelope is held
  at its last row and a long note must not fall off the front of it.

## If you pick this up

1. **The other three channels.** Nothing here uses channels 4 and 5 or
   the second noise generator. A second flute a fifth away, or a drone,
   is the same routine with another set of state bytes.
2. **The envelope generators would buy a better attack** — they run off
   frequency generator 1 or 4, so they modulate at audio rate rather
   than frame rate, and the breathy *muraiki* attack really wants that.
3. **`strings.z80s` is the same machinery** with the channels spent on
   a chord instead of a harmonic stack, and `ensemble.z80s` runs both
   at once on three channels each.

    python3 soundchip/tests/mkshakudata.py    # the tables, from the Hz
    python3 soundchip/tests/test_shaku.py     # verify, time, and write the wav
